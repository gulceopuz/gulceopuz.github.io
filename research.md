## Job Market Paper

### *Portfolio choice with heterogeneous risks*  

Wealthier households allocate a higher percentage of their wealth to equity, a pattern that standard life cycle portfolio choice models cannot explain without decreasing relative risk aversion (DRRA) preferences. While DRRA preferences align with observed data, evidence from long-run consumption and risk premia suggests that relative risk aversion
should not depend on wealth. I propose an alternative explanation: wealthier households benefit from higher Sharpe ratios due to reduced risk through more effective diversification and more accurate return estimates, and are subject to lower participation costs. By incorporating these factors into a life cycle portfolio choice model, I show how these changes bring the model closer to explaining the portfolio allocation decisions of wealthier households. At the aggregate level, heterogeneity in risk, returns and costs jointly raises
the wealth Gini coefficient from 0.63 to 0.73 and the wealth share of the top 10% from 48% to 56% in the United States. [PDF]( /assets/JMP_GO_Sep26.pdf )
